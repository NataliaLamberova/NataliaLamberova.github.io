I am an applied political economist at the University of Texas at Dallas. I study how political institutions, governments, and public preferences shape innovation, information, and technological change. My work combines formal theory with causal inference, experiments, and original data to examine the political foundations of technology and public policy.

Across this research, I ask why governments support some forms of innovation, how institutions affect the quality and direction of technological change, and how citizens interpret information and evaluate long-term public investments.

## Featured research

### Information Manipulation and Repression: Theory and Evidence from the COVID-19 Response in Russia

With Konstantin Sonin. *Journal of Public Economics*, 262 (2026), 105740. The paper studies how information manipulation and repression operate together under authoritarian rule, using evidence from 83 Russian regions. [PDF](/files/Lamberova_Sonin_Information_Manipulation_Repression.pdf) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2026.105740)

### The Paradox of Government-Funded Innovation in Weakly Institutionalized Environments

*Journal of Innovation & Knowledge*, 9(3) (2024), 100536. The paper examines why public support for research can increase patenting while reducing the quality of innovation when institutions provide weak oversight. [PDF](/files/Lamberova_Paradox_Government_Funded_Innovation.pdf) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jik.2024.100536)

### Patent Protection and Innovative Entrepreneurship

With Maxim Ananyev. This working paper uses the *Alice* Supreme Court decision to study how changes in software-patent protection affect startup funding, entry, and the prospect of reaching an IPO. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4739142) · [Research summary](/research/#patent-protection-and-innovative-entrepreneurship)
